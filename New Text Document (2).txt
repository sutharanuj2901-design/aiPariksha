Chapter 1: JEE Main (Paper 1 – B.E./B.Tech)
1.1 Exam Overview
Exam Name	Joint Entrance Examination – Main (JEE Main), Paper 1
Full Form	Joint Entrance Examination Main
Conducting Authority	National Testing Agency (NTA)
Official Website	jeemain.nta.nic.in
Educational Qualification	Passed Class 12 (or equivalent) with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics as compulsory subjects. Candidates appearing in Class 12 in the current or preceding year are also eligible. No minimum percentage is required for JEE Main appearance itself (only for admission eligibility, generally 75% in Class 12, or top 20 percentile of respective board, relaxed for SC/ST to 65%).
Age Limit	No upper age limit.
Attempts Allowed	No lifetime cap on total attempts, but only within 3 consecutive years of passing Class 12; up to 2 sessions (Jan & Apr) per year may be taken.
Important Dates (Typical Yearly Timeline)	Notification: Oct–Nov | Session 1 Registration: Nov–Dec | Session 1 Exam: last week Jan (over multiple shifts/dates) | Session 1 Result: early Feb | Session 2 Registration: Feb–Mar | Session 2 Exam: 1st–2nd week Apr | Session 2 Result & Final Cutoff: mid-to-late Apr | JEE Advanced registration opens: immediately after Session 2 result
Exam Mode	Computer-Based Test (CBT) — fully online
Exam Duration	3 hours (4 hours for PwD candidates with scribe)
Number of Questions	75 (25 each from Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics) — all compulsory, no optional questions since 2024
Question Types	20 MCQs + 5 Numerical Value Questions (NVQs) per subject
Maximum Marks	300
Marking Scheme	+4 for every correct answer
Negative Marking	−1 for every incorrect MCQ; NO negative marking for incorrect Numerical Value Questions (unattempted = 0)
Number of Sessions	2 per year (January and April) — best of the two NTA scores/percentiles is considered
Languages	13 languages: English, Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Odia, Punjabi, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu
1.2 Section-wise Marks Distribution
Subject	MCQs	Numerical (NVQ)	Total Questions	Total Marks
Physics	20	5	25	100
Chemistry	20	5	25	100
Mathematics	20	5	25	100
Total	60	15	75	300
1.3 Sectional Timing & Cutoff
There is no fixed sectional time limit — candidates may allocate the full 3 hours across subjects as they choose. There is no sectional qualifying cutoff for JEE Main; only an overall category-wise percentile cutoff decides eligibility for JEE Advanced. However, a balanced subject-wise attempt (ideally 55–65 minutes per subject) is recommended by experts for optimum accuracy.
1.4 Complete Exam Pattern
•	Paper delivered as Computer Based Test (CBT) on desktop/laptop terminals at designated centres.
•	Section A (MCQs): single correct answer out of 4 options; +4/−1.
•	Section B (Numerical Value): answer entered as a numerical value (integer/decimal) using an on-screen numeric keypad; +4/0 (no negative marking).
•	All 5 NVQs per subject are compulsory (optional-attempt format used in 2020–2023 was discontinued from 2024 onwards).
•	No use of physical/virtual calculator, log tables, or slide rules is permitted; a virtual on-screen calculator/rough sheet is provided for basic arithmetic in certain years — candidates must verify latest NTA notice.
•	Normalisation: Since the exam is held over multiple shifts/dates, NTA converts raw marks into a percentile score (NTA Score) separately for each shift to ensure fairness; All India Rank (AIR) is based on this percentile, not raw marks.
•	Best of two sessions (Jan/Apr) is used for final AIR and JEE Advanced eligibility.

1.7 Difficulty Level
Moderate to moderately-difficult. Physics and Mathematics are generally rated as lengthier/calculation-intensive; Chemistry (especially Inorganic and NCERT-based Physical/Organic factual recall) is considered the most scoring and time-efficient section. Overall difficulty is calibrated by NTA to be lower than JEE Advanced but higher than most state-level engineering exams.

1.11 Preparation Strategy
•	Master NCERT thoroughly before moving to reference books — over 70% of Chemistry and a meaningful share of Physics questions are directly NCERT-rooted.
•	Build a chapter-wise formula sheet for Physics and Mathematics for quick daily revision.
•	Practice previous 10–15 years' papers (JEE Main + Advanced) topic-wise to identify recurring question patterns.
•	Take one full-length mock test weekly under strict 3-hour timed conditions to build stamina and speed.
•	Prioritise accuracy in NVQs since there is no negative marking, but do not guess in MCQs blindly given the −1 penalty.
•	Maintain an error log to track and eliminate recurring mistakes.
1.12 Recommended Study Plan (12-Month Structure, for Class 12 aspirants)
Phase	Duration	Focus
Foundation	Months 1–4	Complete NCERT + basic theory of all Class 11 & 12 chapters; concept-building exercises
Application	Months 5–8	Chapter-wise problem solving from reference books; topic tests; start previous-year question practice
Integration	Months 9–10	Full-syllabus revision; mixed topic tests; timed sectional tests
Mock & Revision	Months 11–12	Full-length mock tests (2–3/week), error-log review, formula revision, light approach in final 10 days before exam
 
Chapter 2: JEE Main Paper 2A (B.Arch)
2.1 Exam Overview
Exam Name	JEE Main Paper 2A
Full Form	Joint Entrance Examination Main — Paper 2A (Bachelor of Architecture)
Conducting Authority	National Testing Agency (NTA)
Official Website	jeemain.nta.nic.in
Educational Qualification	Passed Class 12 with Physics, Chemistry and Mathematics; minimum 50% aggregate marks in Class 12 (or equivalent) including Mathematics as a subject
Important Dates	Conducted alongside JEE Main Session 1 (Jan) and Session 2 (Apr) cycle; typically one shift/day common for both Paper 2A and 2B
Exam Mode	Hybrid — Mathematics & Aptitude sections: CBT (online); Drawing Test: pen-and-paper (offline)
Exam Duration	3 hours
Number of Questions	77 (Mathematics: 25 [20 MCQ + 5 NVQ]; Aptitude: 50 MCQ; Drawing: 2 questions)
Maximum Marks	400
Marking Scheme	+4 for every correct MCQ/NVQ in Maths & Aptitude; Drawing evaluated out of 100 marks on artistic/technical merit
Negative Marking	−1 for incorrect MCQs in Mathematics & Aptitude; no negative marking for NVQs or Drawing
2.2 Section-wise Marks Distribution
Section	Subject	Questions	Marks
Part I	Mathematics (20 MCQ + 5 NVQ)	25	100
Part II	Aptitude Test	50	200
Part III	Drawing Test	2	100
Total	—	77	400
2.3 Sectional Timing
No individually enforced sectional time limits within the 3-hour window; however, candidates typically allocate approx. 45–60 minutes each to Mathematics and Aptitude and the remaining time to the two Drawing questions.
2.4 Complete Exam Pattern
•	Mathematics and Aptitude sections are computer-based (CBT); Drawing Test is conducted offline on a separate answer sheet using pencil/colour.
•	Aptitude section tests awareness of national/international architects and buildings, visualising 3D objects from 2D drawings, and general mental ability.
•	Drawing Test evaluates sketching of a given scene/object from memory, sense of proportion, perspective drawing, colour scheme, and object composition.
2.5 Complete Syllabus
Mathematics
•	Same syllabus as JEE Main Paper 1 Mathematics (see Chapter 1, Section 1.5)
Aptitude Test
•	Awareness of persons, places, buildings, and materials
•	Texture related to architecture and build environment
•	Visualising three-dimensional objects from two-dimensional drawings
•	Visualising different sides of 3D objects
•	Analytical reasoning and mental ability (visual, numerical and verbal)
Drawing Test
•	Sketching of a given scene or activity from memory (urban scenes, landscapes, nature)
•	Transformation of 2D to 3D shapes and vice-versa
•	Development of surfaces and volumes of geometrical/abstract shapes and objects
•	Generation of plan, elevation, and 3D views of objects
•	Creation of 2D and 3D union, intersection of rotated/scaled geometrical shapes
•	Colour texture, harmony and contrast
2.6 Weightage & Difficulty
Mathematics section is considered as difficult as JEE Main Paper 1 Maths. Aptitude section rewards general awareness and spatial visualisation; is high-scoring with practice. Drawing Test difficulty is subjective/skill-based and rewards regular sketching practice and originality.
2.7 Previous Year Trend Analysis
•	Drawing prompts have consistently focused on everyday urban/rural scenes, monuments, and nature-based compositions.
•	Aptitude questions increasingly test 3D visualization and figure-counting/mental-rotation type questions.
2.8 Best Books
•	A Complete Self-Study Guide for B.Arch – P.K. Mishra
•	Steps to Architecture — Corner Stone / GKP publications
•	NATA & B.Arch Drawing practice workbooks
•	NCERT Mathematics XI & XII
2.9 Preparation Strategy & Study Plan
•	Dedicate daily practice (30–45 min) purely to freehand sketching and perspective drawing.
•	Solve Mathematics using the same plan as JEE Main Paper 1.
•	Regularly study iconic Indian and global architectural structures for Aptitude awareness questions.
•	Practice previous 8–10 years' Paper 2A question papers under timed conditions.
2.10 Previous Year Cutoff Trend (NIT/SPA B.Arch, General Category, Indicative)
B.Arch admission cutoffs are institute- and course-specific (declared via JoSAA/CSAB counselling) and are expressed as percentile/rank rather than a single national qualifying score. Competition for NITs' B.Arch programmes (limited to ~7 NITs) is intense, with top-NIT closing ranks typically within the top 2,000–3,000 among Paper 2A candidates in recent cycles.

Mathematics carries similar difficulty to Paper 1. The Planning section is largely GK/awareness-based and rewards current-affairs reading, particularly urban policy, sustainability, and infrastructure news. Aptitude overlaps significantly with Paper 2A.

3.8 Previous Year Cutoff Trend
B.Planning is offered at a very limited number of institutes (a few NITs and the Schools of Planning and Architecture — SPA Delhi, Bhopal, Vijayawada), making seats extremely limited (well under 500 nationally) and cutoffs highly competitive relative to the small applicant pool for this paper.
 
Chapter 4: JEE Advanced
4.1 Exam Overview
Exam Name	Joint Entrance Examination – Advanced (JEE Advanced)
Full Form	Joint Entrance Examination Advanced
Conducting Authority	One of the seven zonal IITs on rotation, under the Joint Admission Board (JAB) — 2026 edition conducted by IIT Roorkee
Official Website	jeeadv.ac.in
Educational Qualification	Must rank within the top 2,50,000 candidates (across all categories) in JEE Main Paper 1; passed Class 12 in the current or preceding year (with limited relaxations); maximum of 2 attempts in 2 consecutive years
Important Dates (Typical Timeline)	JEE Advanced registration opens: within days of JEE Main Session 2 result (late Apr) | Registration closes: early May | Exam: mid-May | Provisional answer key: ~1 week after exam | Result: early June | JoSAA counselling: June–July (multiple rounds)
Exam Mode	Computer-Based Test (CBT), fully online
Exam Duration	2 papers of 3 hours each, held on the same day (Paper 1: forenoon; Paper 2: afternoon) — total 6 hours
Number of Questions	Varies year to year (typically 51 per paper; ~102 total) — exact pattern is not fixed and changes annually
Maximum Marks	Varies yearly (typically 360 total, ~180 per paper) — official pattern released only via the exam's own instructions on exam day
Marking Scheme	Mixed — full marks for fully correct answers; partial marks in certain multi-correct/multiple-answer questions; varies by question type each year
Negative Marking	Present in most MCQ-type questions (typically −1 or −2 depending on question type); NO negative marking on Numerical Answer Type questions
4.2 Section-wise Structure (Representative Pattern — Varies Yearly)
Paper	Subjects Covered	Approx. Duration	Approx. Max Marks
Paper 1	Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics (mixed question types: single-correct, multi-correct, numerical)	3 hours	~180
Paper 2	Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics (different question formats — comprehension-based, matrix-match, numerical)	3 hours	~180
Note: JEE Advanced deliberately varies its exact question count, marks-per-question, and negative-marking rules every year to prevent pattern-based rote preparation. Candidates must rely on the official mock test and instructions released a few days before the exam for the exact year's scheme.
4.3 Complete Exam Pattern (General Structure)
•	Two compulsory papers on the same day; both must be attempted for the result to be considered.
•	Question types across recent years have included: Single Correct MCQs, Multiple Correct MCQs (partial marking), Numerical Answer Type (decimal/integer, no negative marking), Matrix-Match/List-Match, and Paragraph/Comprehension-based sets.
•	Rank list (Common Rank List, CRL) is based on aggregate marks in both papers combined; subject-wise and category-wise ranks are also declared.
•	A minimum aggregate marks criterion AND a minimum per-subject marks criterion (each generally ~5–10% of subject maximum) must both be cleared to be included in the merit list.
•	Questions are available in English and Hindi.
4.4 Complete Subject-wise Syllabus
The JEE Advanced syllabus largely mirrors JEE Main but is tested at a significantly higher conceptual depth, with heavier emphasis on multi-concept application, calculation-intensive problems, and topics requiring rigorous derivation.
Physics (Class 11 + 12, same broad chapters as JEE Main with deeper treatment)
•	General Physics — units, dimensions, experimental error analysis
•	Mechanics — kinematics, Newton's laws, work-energy-power, centre of mass, rotational dynamics, gravitation, elasticity, hydrostatics/hydrodynamics
•	Thermal Physics — thermal expansion, calorimetry, heat transfer, kinetic theory, thermodynamics laws
•	Waves & Oscillations — SHM, string/sound waves, Doppler effect
•	Electricity & Magnetism — electrostatics, current electricity, magnetic effects, EMI, AC circuits
•	Optics — geometrical optics, wave optics, optical instruments
•	Modern Physics — photoelectric effect, atomic structure, radioactivity, nuclear physics
Chemistry
•	Physical Chemistry — mole concept, gaseous/liquid states, thermodynamics, chemical/ionic equilibrium, electrochemistry, chemical kinetics, solid state, surface chemistry
•	Inorganic Chemistry — periodic table, chemical bonding, hydrogen, s/p/d/f-block elements, coordination compounds, extractive metallurgy, qualitative analysis
•	Organic Chemistry — IUPAC nomenclature, isomerism, reaction mechanisms, hydrocarbons, haloalkanes/haloarenes, alcohols/phenols/ethers, aldehydes/ketones, carboxylic acids, amines, biomolecules, polymers, practical/qualitative organic analysis
Mathematics
•	Algebra — quadratic equations, sequences & series, permutations & combinations, binomial theorem, complex numbers, matrices & determinants, probability
•	Trigonometry — identities, equations, inverse functions, properties of triangles
•	Analytical/Coordinate Geometry — straight lines, circles, conic sections, 3D geometry
•	Differential Calculus — functions, limits, continuity, differentiability, application of derivatives
•	Integral Calculus — definite/indefinite integration, area under curves, differential equations
•	Vectors
4.5 Weightage of Important Chapters
Subject	Consistently High-Weightage Topics

4.6 Difficulty Level
Very high — widely regarded as one of the toughest undergraduate entrance examinations in the world. Questions demand multi-step reasoning, combine concepts across chapters, and are calculation-intensive under significant time pressure.

•	2025 saw a notable pattern change introduced by IIT Kanpur; each conducting IIT tends to bring its own stylistic tweaks.
•	2026 (IIT Roorkee): 1,79,694 candidates appeared; 56,880 qualified — a qualifying rate of roughly 31–32%.
•	Physics numericals and multi-concept Mechanics/Electrodynamics problems remain persistently high-weightage across years.
